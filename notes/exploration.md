# Intelligence Engine: exploration notes

A running record of what's been thought through so far. **Nothing here is decided.**
These are hypotheses, observations and open questions, kept so the pieces aren't lost.

---

## 0. The vision (founder's words)

> Something that started very simple and kept evolving, compounding, eventually a universal
> intelligence engine at civilisational level that pushes the boundaries of frontiers and
> discovery. Collective intelligence, getting better and compounding, for **truth seeking,
> liberation, profoundness, expanding the horizons of intelligence.**

Note: the thinking narrowed too far at one point (seed → double-entry → verification → checking
a stock count). Verification is defensive; the systems admired here (evolution, markets, shared
fictions) are mainly **generative**. Keep both halves in view.

**Liberation** = "liberation from whatever binds." Open-ended, like the engine. Candidate bindings:
ignorance, scarcity, the limits of a single mind, error and illusion, failures to cooperate,
old frames of thought, domination by others. Tension: every great liberating system has also
bound people (markets, religion, science, the internet). Open question: how does the engine stay
liberating as it grows, and never become the thing that binds?

## The broken thing, in the founder's words (first contact with real experience)

> It's all there in digital records, in the cloud or my computer history. Everything has logs.
> AI can just be persistent enough and know what I want (intent) without me saying it again and
> again each and every time. It not doing it is frustrating. Just be my shadow and work for me.
> Spend my money, log into my accounts, ask me questions, but have the agency to actually really help me.

Parts of it: **persistence** (doesn't start from zero), **intent** (knows what I want),
**access** (the records already exist), **agency** (acts, not just advises), **initiative**
(asks questions), **working on my behalf** (a shadow).

**Would you let it spend your money?** "Yes, but with a little hesitation, and a limited sum.
I would need it to be reliable enough." This is how people already delegate: a small limit
that grows as trust is earned (new employees, credit limits). Possible loop, from the founder's
own answers: record of what it did → track record → trust → higher limit → more it can do
for you → more record. A candidate for what compounds. Not yet tested.

**What is "reliable enough"?** "That it broadly serves my intent (implicit or explicit).
If it broadly serves me, that will likely satisfy me." Notes: (a) the judge here is the
person's intent, not only reality; earlier "reality is the only judge" was incomplete.
(b) "Broadly": judged on the whole, not on every step; perfection isn't required.
(c) Implicit intent may be learnable from reactions over time (what gets approved,
changed, rejected or ignored), the way prices reveal preferences.

## 1. Starting hypotheses (the founder's)

- All economically valuable work is either **information processing** or **physical work**.
  Most physical work is already done by machines, and many of those are run by code.
- Information processing is either **logic-based** ("if this, then that": code, calculation)
  or **probabilistic** (thinking, judgment). The probabilistic kind is now moving to AI too.
- There are two layers: the **model layer** (capital- and talent-heavy, out of reach) and the
  **system layer** (what's built around models).
- Much of the system layer gets made redundant as models improve. But some systems survive
  and scale beyond any individual: free markets, evolution, law, democracy, money, companies,
  shared beliefs. They feel "Spinoza-like": no central designer, just order that emerges.
- The surviving part of the system layer compounds over time, and deserves attention and investment.
- Model-agnostic, general systems may be possible.

## 2. The motivation

- With every model release, **something fundamental still feels broken** that better models
  don't fix. It hasn't been named yet.
- AI may take over nearly all economic tasks, while ownership stays with humans.
  What's the durable, valuable thing to build in that world?
- The aspiration: an engine that **doesn't need to be told what to do, does it reliably and
  correctly, keeps improving, and doesn't need its owner.**

## 3. The story (a lens, not a proof)

Evolution (no minds, memory in genes) → brains (learning in a lifetime) → language
(knowledge outlives the knower) → writing and shared fictions (strangers cooperate;
individual brains barely changed, what grew was *between* them) → machines take muscle →
computers take logic → AI takes probabilistic thinking.

**Observed pattern:** every new kind of mind eventually got a new kind of system around it.
The system didn't make individuals smarter. It remembered, connected, selected and gave direction.

**This chapter is odd:** a new mind has arrived, and its system hasn't been invented yet.
Today's harnesses may be scaffolding around a child, which is why they keep being outgrown.

## 4. Four jobs those systems did

1. **Remember** beyond the individual
2. **Connect** individuals
3. **Select** what works
4. **Give direction**

No single human system did all four. Today's AI has almost none of them.

## 5. Seeds

The lasting systems weren't designed whole. They grew from **small seeds**: double-entry
bookkeeping, the stamped coin, the scientific journal, the joint-stock company, the internet's rules.

What the seeds seem to share:
- Tiny: explainable in a sentence
- Rules about *how*, not instructions about *what*
- **Useful to the very first user, alone**
- More valuable with time and with more users
- Indifferent to who uses them (agnostic)
- **Made something visible that was invisible before**, and trust grew from what could be seen

Guiding question: **what is the double-entry bookkeeping of this era?**

## 6. Verification (latest thread)

- Double-entry's power was **verifiability**: errors showed themselves. It didn't make
  merchants smarter; it changed the *form of the record*.
- It only checked a narrow property (consistency), not whether the business was run well.
  A narrow, cheap, reliable check may be enough to seed a lot.
- Ways humans verify open-ended judgment: **two independent paths agree**; **record the claim
  now, let reality settle it later**; **trusted review** (reputation built on the first two).
- Where the double-entry parallel breaks: it was cheap, conclusive, and rested on a
  **conservation law** (money isn't created by recording it). Thinking has no such law.
- The only "conserved" thing found so far: **reality doesn't contradict itself.**
  - Where probabilistic work touches reality (facts, numbers, events, outcomes), verification is possible.
  - Where it doesn't (values, taste, strategy, purpose), it stays judgment, possibly
    where humans remain essential.

## 7. Things currently invisible when working with AI

- Whether it was right
- Where it went wrong (mistakes vanish with the conversation)
- What it learned from you (teaching disappears)
- How much to trust it, on what (lives only in your head)
- Why it did what it did

Common thread: things that happened but left no trace.

## 8. Considered, not settled

- **The shape of the engine.** A free market was the founder's gut pick, but other shapes may
  fit as well or better: evolution, a company (a legal fiction), science, a single growing mind.
  What drew the founder to the market isn't yet known.
- **Is the broken thing trust and delegation?** Suggested, not confirmed.
- **Many agents, or one entity that grows?** Open.
- **Starting inside the founder's own business?** Suggested, not agreed.

## 9. Open questions

- Does the pattern "new mind → new system" hold when the mind improves in months, not centuries?
- Who are the members of the new system: humans, AIs, both? Humans as owners? Judges? Customers?
- What is the equivalent of "writing" for this mind: what does it need outside itself?
- Could the bitter lesson eat even markets? (Hayek vs. central planning, revisited for superintelligence.)
- What is the "price signal" for intelligence, the one number that lets strangers decide whom to trust?
- When the engine doesn't need its owner for the *doing*, what keeps it serving humans?
- What seed is useful on day one to one person, grows with time, ignores which model is used,
  and makes something invisible visible?
